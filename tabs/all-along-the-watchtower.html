Song Journey

All Along the Watchtower

Bob Dylan · made famous by Jimi Hendrix
🧵 Thread song — it appears across all 5 modules 0 of 5 layers rated

One song, five layers. Every module this semester, you come back to this song with a new skill — and it gets bigger every time.

Song map: the whole song is one three-chord loop — Am · G · F · G — repeating under every verse and every solo. Dylan sings over it; Hendrix solos over it. Everything below is that loop at a different depth.
All layers are teaching arrangements at 60 BPM — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions.

This track runs at 115 BPM, two beats per chord — the record's real cycle, faster than your 60 BPM practice layers. Not there yet? Toggle 🐢 Slow (90 BPM) for a stepping stone before the full tempo.

This song exists twice: Bob Dylan's 1967 original and Jimi Hendrix's 1968 cover — same three chords, very different guitars. Before you play a note, hear what one arrangement choice (acoustic vs. electric) does to the same song.

Do it: listen to the opening minute of both versions. One guitar is acoustic, one is electric — decide which is which, and name one word for how each version feels.
Respond: Dylan wrote it — so why do most people know the Hendrix version? What did the cover add?
Connect: name another song where a cover became more famous than the original.

Three notes on the low E string carry this whole song: the roots of the chord loop. This riff (a short musical phrase that repeats) was your Module 2 assessment piece — the goal is playing it from memory, eyes closed.

Bass loop — low E string · one note per beat
        A    G    F    G    (loop)
e |--------------------------------------|
B |--------------------------------------|
G |--------------------------------------|
D |--------------------------------------|
A |--------------------------------------|
E |--5----3----1----3--------------------|
Play it: one note per beat at 60 BPM, four laps (a lap = one full time through the loop) without stopping. Fingering: index on fret 1 (F), ring on fret 3 (G), pinky on fret 5 (A) — no jumping, every finger has a home.
Stuck?
Loop just F → G (frets 1–3) until the change is clean, then add the A on fret 5.
Level up
Say each note name out loud as you play, or run the whole loop with your eyes closed.

Same three roots you played in Layer 2 — each note now also plays the note a fifth above it. One two-string shape sliding along the low E string.

        A5   G5   F5   G5   (loop)
e |----------------------------------------|
B |----------------------------------------|
G |----------------------------------------|
D |----------------------------------------|
A |--7----5----3----5----------------------|
E |--5----3----1----3----------------------|
Play it: two beats per chord at 60 BPM, four clean laps, every change landing on the beat. Keep your grip — it's one shape, your arm does the moving.
Stuck?
Drop to roots-only (your Layer 2 line) for a lap, then add the second string back on just the A5.
Level up
One beat per chord — same 60 BPM, twice the changes — or palm-mute the verse (rest the side of your strumming hand on the strings for a short, muffled sound), then let the strings ring louder for a big lap.

The loop is in A minor, so A minor pentatonic Pattern 1 fits every bar — this is the fret shape Hendrix used for most of his solos. Start with three notes only and leave gaps of silence between notes — the space is part of the music.

Your three starting notes — Am pentatonic Pattern 1 · 5th position
        E        G         A
e |----------------------------------|
B |----------------------------------|
G |----------------------------------|
D |--------------5---------7---------|
A |--7-------------------------------|
E |----------------------------------|
Play it: loop the chords in your head (or with a partner on Layer 3) and improvise 4 bars on those three notes, then 4 more adding one note of your choice from Pattern 1. Start or end every phrase on A — that's 'home', the note the music rests on.
Stuck?
Quarter notes only, two-beat phrases, two-beat rests — until an idea shows up on its own.
Level up
Start a phrase during the F bar and resolve it on the Am bar — that's real tension and release.

The simple singalong version — closest to how Dylan actually plays it. The same loop you've played as bass notes and power chords becomes full open chords you can strum and sing over. Use the small F (top four strings, xx3211) — no barre needed (a barre presses one finger flat across several strings).

Singalong loop — 2 beats per chord · 60 BPM
|  Am        G        |  F         G        |  (loop)
   1    2    3    4      1    2    3    4
Play it: down-strums, two beats per chord, four loops — and listen for it: this is the SAME song you played in Modules 2 and 3, finally sounding full.
Stuck?
Strum only beat 1 of each chord and use the silent beats to travel to the next shape.
Level up
Trade loops with a partner playing Layer 3 power chords — Dylan and Hendrix at the same time — or hum a verse over your own strumming.
