One song, five layers. Every module this semester, you come back to this song with a new skill — and it gets bigger every time. There's also a bonus layer — the solo, the riff an octave up (Layer 6) — once you're ready for a harder challenge.

Song map: Intro / Verse (riff ×4, quiet) → Chorus (same riff, big) → Solo section (riff keeps looping underneath). The whole song is one seven-note idea: E · E · G · E · D · C · B. Everything below is that idea at a different depth.
All layers are teaching arrangements at 60 BPM — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions.

This track runs at record speed (123 BPM) — the vocals and full band are here, but the guitar riff is dialed down so you can play it live over the mix. Much faster than the 60 BPM layers below, and the riff swings looser than the straight quarters you've been practicing. New: the 🐢 Slow toggle gives you a 100 BPM stepping stone.

Before you play a note, hear what makes this riff (a short musical phrase that repeats) work: it moves in one direction (down), and it leaves space. Listen once for the riff, once for the drums, once for how the chorus gets bigger without adding new notes.

Do it: listen to the song once through. Count how many times the riff repeats before the first chorus.
Respond: is the guitar sound clean or distorted? Does it change between verse and chorus? Answer in a frame: "The guitar sound is ___ because ___."
Connect: name one other song built on a short repeating riff — "___ does the same thing."
Check it when: you've answered the questions and can hum the main hook from memory.

3
Hammer-ons and pull-offs
Same box, two new moves. A hammer-on (h): pick the first note, then tap the higher fret hard with your ring finger — two notes, one pick. A pull-off (p): pick the fretted note, then flick that finger off the string so the open string sounds — two notes, one pick. Work each move slowly until it speaks, then sneak them into your three-note phrases — suddenly you sound like a record.

The simple singalong version. The riff's notes become a chord loop you can strum and sing over — a teaching arrangement built from chords you know.

Play it: down-strums, two beats per chord, every change landing on its beat. Hum the riff while you strum — same notes, bigger sound.
Stuck?
Strum only beat 1 of each chord and travel on the silent beats.
Level up
Split strum (down · down-up) on each chord, or trade loops with a partner playing Layer 3 power chords — together you're the whole band. Or try the bonus Layer 6 below — the solo, one octave up.

Bonus layer — optional harder challenge, no score. Best once the Layer 2 riff plays from memory.

Jack White's solo is a trick hiding in plain sight: it's the main riff again, an octave up, screamed out on the thinnest string with a slide (a metal or glass tube worn on a finger). You don't need a slide — your fingers already know the moves, because the frets are your Layer 2 frets plus five.

Play it: one note per beat at 60 BPM on the high e string alone — the same pattern as Layer 2, five frets up: 12 · 12 · 15 · 12, then 10 · 8 · 7. Slide your finger between frets instead of lifting it and it starts to cry like the record.
Stuck?
Play Layer 2's A-string version once, then this one — say the note names as you go: they're identical (E · E · G · E · D · C · B). Your ears already know the path.
Level up
Trade with a partner: one plays the low riff (Layer 2 or 3), one answers up here — that's the whole band. Or slide INTO fret 12 from two frets below on each E — the classic slide-guitar entrance.
