Song Journey

Seven Nation Army

The White Stripes
🧵 Thread song — it appears in all 5 units

One song, five layers. Every unit this semester, you come back to this song with a new skill — and it gets bigger every time. Find your current layer below. Done early? Move down one layer and try the next.

Song map: Intro / Verse (riff ×4, quiet) → Chorus (same riff, big) → Solo section (riff keeps looping underneath). The whole song is one seven-note idea: E · E · G · E · D · C · B. Everything below is that idea at a different depth.
All layers are teaching arrangements at 60 BPM — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions.
1

Listen

Unit 1

Before you play a note, hear what makes this riff (a short musical phrase that repeats) work: it moves in one direction (down), and it leaves space. Listen once for the riff, once for the drums, once for how the chorus gets bigger without adding new notes.

Do it: listen to the song once through. Count how many times the riff repeats before the first chorus.
Respond: is the guitar sound clean or distorted? Does it change between verse and chorus?
Connect: name one other song built on a short repeating riff.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid
2

Single Notes

Unit 2

You can play the riff in two places. You learned it first on the low E string (easy reach, one octave down). At true pitch it sits on the A string — that version sounds like the original recording.

Where you started — low E string (octave down)

B |--------------------------------------|

        E    E    G    E    D    C    B
e |--------------------------------------|
B |--------------------------------------|
G |--------------------------------------|
D |--------------------------------------|
A |--7----7----10---7----5----3----2-----|
E |--------------------------------------|
Play it: one note per beat at 60 BPM, both positions, without looking at the chart. Say the note names out loud — they change between positions even though the moves don't.
Stuck? Two halves: E E G E, then D C B. Join them when each is automatic.
Level up: alternate laps (a lap = one full time through the riff) between the two positions without stopping.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid
3

Power Chords

Unit 3

Same riff you played as single notes — now every root becomes a two-string power chord, one shape sliding along the A string.

        E5   E5   G5   E5   D5   C5   B5
e |----------------------------------------|
B |----------------------------------------|
G |----------------------------------------|
D |--9----9----12---9----7----5----4-----|
A |--7----7----10---7----5----3----2-----|
E |----------------------------------------|
Play it: one chord per riff note at 60 BPM, every string of every chord ringing clean. Palm-mute the verse (rest the side of your strumming hand on the strings for a short, muffled sound), then let the strings ring louder in the chorus.
Stuck? Play roots-only (your Layer 2 line) and add the second string back one chord at a time.
Level up: ◐ You can do the same thing on “the cure” — gentler song, same shapes.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid
4

Pentatonic Solo

Unit 4

The riff is in E minor, so E minor pentatonic fits over every bar. Start with three notes only — Rule of 3 — and leave gaps of silence between notes: the space is part of the music.

Your three starting notes — E minor pentatonic, open position
        E         G         A
e |----------------------------------|
B |----------------------------------|
G |----------------------------------|
D |----------------------------------|
A |--------------------0-------------|
E |--0---------3---------------------|
Play it: loop the riff in your head (or with a partner) and improvise 4 bars on those three notes. End every phrase on E — that's 'home', the note the music rests on.
Stuck? Quarter notes only, two-beat phrases, two-beat rests.
Level up: move the same shape to the 12th fret — the higher pitch range used for solos.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid
5

Open Chords

Unit 5

The simple singalong version. The riff's notes become a chord loop you can strum and sing over — a teaching arrangement built from chords you know.

Singalong loop — 2 beats per chord · 60 BPM
|  Em   Em  |  G    Em  |  D    C   |  C    (rest) |
   1    2      3    4      1    2      3    4
Play it: down-strums, two beats per chord, every change landing on its beat. Hum the riff while you strum — same notes, bigger sound.
Stuck? Strum only beat 1 of each chord and travel on the silent beats.
Level up: split strum (down · down-up) on each chord, or trade loops with a partner playing Layer 3 power chords — together you're the whole band.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid
