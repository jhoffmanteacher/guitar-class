One song, five layers. As your skills grow this semester, you come back to this song with a new tool — and it gets bigger every time. There's also a bonus Layer 6 with the song's fingerpicked intro, once you're ready for a harder challenge.

Song map: the whole song rides F · Am; Dm makes a brief passing appearance near the end of the verse and again in the closing bridge (some charts voice it as Dm9 there). It's in 6/8 — feel the pulse in 2 and strum the downbeats.
More about this song

Peso Pluma and Junior H's "Luna" is one of the biggest hits of its time in the sierreño style (a modern Mexican acoustic style), sung over just an acoustic requinto (a small, higher-pitched guitar that plays the lead melody) and bass — which is why every layer here builds toward playing it acoustic, without an amplifier.

All layers are teaching arrangements — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions. Tempo targets stay on the usual steps — 60, then 70, then 80 downbeat BPM — even though the original recording moves faster in 6/8.

This track runs at record speed (128 BPM), felt in 2 — same two-downbeat-strums-per-bar feel you've been playing since Module 3, just at the record's real tempo.

Before you play a note, feel where the pulse lives. "Luna" is in 6/8 — count it out and you might land on 2 big beats or 6 small ones, and both are true at once: two big beats, each one dividing into three. Tap along with just the two big beats and let the requinto play the rest.

Do it: listen to the first verse. Tap only the two big downbeats per bar — try not to tap all six.
Respond: what does the requinto add to the song that strumming alone can't?
Connect: name another song where an acoustic guitar carries the hook (the catchy part you remember), not just the rhythm.

The whole song as open chords: F (the little shape, xx3211) and Am carry it start to finish as one vamp (a short chord pattern repeated over and over), with Dm making a brief passing appearance near the end of the verse and again in the closing bridge. Same 6/8 pulse — two downbeat strums per bar, nothing syncopated.

Verse vamp — 2 downbeats per bar · 6/8 felt in 2 · 60 BPM
|  F      |  Am     |  F      |  Am     |
   1  2      1  2      1  2      1  2
Play it: down-strums, two beats per bar, four laps with every change landing on the downbeat and the little F ringing clean. Near the end of the verse and in the closing bridge, swap in Dm.
Stuck?
Loop just F → Am until the shape change is automatic, then add the metronome at 60 BPM.
Level up
Sing or hum "Luna, dile tú" over the loop, or push to a full verse and chorus without stopping. Module 7 upgrades the little F to the full barre (one finger pressed flat across several strings) — the last beginner shortcut before the real shape. Or try the bonus Layer 6 below for the song's fingerpicked intro.

Bonus layer — optional harder challenge, no score. Ready whenever you are; no specific Module required.

Back in Layer 1 you heard the requinto open the song before the vamp starts. It's rolling through the same little F shape you already know from Layer 5 (xx3211) — just leave the high e string open, and pick the notes one at a time instead of strumming them together.

Play it: pick F, D string fret 3 → A, G string fret 2 → C, B string fret 1 → open e, one note at a time, letting each ring into the next until all four are singing together. Repeat twice, then let the chord ring out before the vamp comes in.
Stuck?
Fret the little F shape first (D3, G2, B1, e open) and strum it once to hear the target chord — then break it apart one string at a time, slowest possible tempo.
Level up
Once the roll is smooth, drop it in front of the Layer 5 vamp as a real intro, or reverse the roll (high to low) for a variation.
