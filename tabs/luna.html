Song Journey

Luna

Peso Pluma & Junior H
🧵 Thread song — it grows through Units 1–5

One song, five layers. As your skills grow this semester, you come back to this song with a new tool — and it gets bigger every time. Find your current layer below. Fast finisher? Scroll down one layer and try the next.

Song map: the whole song rides F · Am; the chorus adds Dm · C. It's in 6/8 — feel the pulse in 2 and strum the downbeats. Peso Pluma and Junior H's "Luna" is one of the biggest sierreño-corridos hits of the era, sung over just an acoustic requinto and bass — which is why every layer here builds toward playing it clean and unplugged.
All layers are teaching arrangements — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions. Tempo targets stay on the usual 60/70/80 downbeat BPM ladder, even though the record itself moves faster in 6/8.
1

Listening

Unit 1

Before you play a note, feel where the pulse lives. "Luna" is in 6/8 — count it out and you might land on 2 big beats or 6 small ones, and both are true at once: two big beats, each one dividing into three. Tap along with just the two big beats and let the requinto fill in the rest.

Do it: listen to the first verse. Tap only the two big downbeats per bar — resist the urge to tap all six.
Respond: what does the requinto (the melodic acoustic guitar) add to the song that strumming alone can't?
Connect: name another song where an acoustic guitar carries the hook, not just the rhythm.
Rate this layer: 1 getting started · 2 steady · 3 solid

Single Notes

Unit 2

Luna rides two chords — F and Am — so its bassline is just two notes: F (low E string, fret 1) and A (open A string). Two big beats each, right on the downbeats.

Bass roots — teaching arrangement · 2 big beats per note

Play it: two big beats per note at 60 BPM, four laps, F ringing with no buzz.
Stuck? Park on just the F — pluck, listen, adjust — until five ring in a row, then add the open A.
Level up: the simplified requinto intro line lands here soon — for now, hunt it by ear against the record.

Power Chords  ◐

Unit 3
◐ Against the grain: this sierreño ballad isn't usually played with power chords — that's the point. Rendering it with distortion goes against the grain of the song on purpose; optional stretch, no score.

Same two notes from Layer 2 — now each one carries its fifth along. F5 and A5, the same two-finger shape sliding four frets.

Play it: two downbeat strums per bar at 60 BPM — nothing syncopated, changing every bar.
Stuck? Park on the slide: fret 1, fret 5, fret 1, fret 5 with no rhythm until the jump is automatic.
Level up: palm-mute for a tight sierreño chug, then let it ring and hear why distortion isn't this song's home.

Pentatonic Solo

Unit 4

Luna lives in F major, and F's relative minor is D — so D minor pentatonic Pattern 1 (root D, low E fret 10) is your box, parked at the semester's highest position.

Your three starting notes — D minor pentatonic, Pattern 1 (10th position)

Play it: improvise over the chord loop — hit ▶ 🎵 Backing track on the Luna card in Module 4 to jam over. End every phrase on D so each idea arrives home.
Stuck? Trade just D and F (frets 10 and 13 on the low E) back and forth, changing only the rhythm, until an idea appears.
Level up: lean on the F (fret 13) for extra sierreño ache, or slide the whole box down to open position and compare the colour.

5

Open Chords

Unit 5

The whole song as open chords: F (the little shape, xx3211) and Am carry the verse, with Dm and C arriving in the chorus. Same 6/8 pulse — two downbeat strums per bar, nothing syncopated.

Verse vamp — 2 downbeats per bar · 6/8 felt in 2 · 60 BPM
|  F      |  Am     |  F      |  Am     |
   1  2      1  2      1  2      1  2
Play it: down-strums, two beats per bar, four laps with every change landing on the downbeat and the little F ringing clean. In the chorus, swap in Dm · C.
Stuck? Loop just F → Am until the shape change is automatic, then add the metronome at 60 BPM.
Level up: sing or hum "Luna, dile tú" over the loop, or push to a full verse and chorus without stopping. Module 7 upgrades the little F to the full barre — Luna's last training wheel.
