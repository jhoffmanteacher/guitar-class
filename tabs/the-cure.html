"the cure"

Olivia Rodrigo
🧵 Thread song — it appears across all 5 modules 0 of 5 layers ready

One song, five layers. Every module this semester, you come back to this song with a new skill — and each time you play a fuller version of it. There's also a bonus Layer 6 with the song's fingerpicked verse, once you're ready for a harder challenge.

Song map: Verse (soft, guitar carries it) → Chorus (fuller, and the chords change) → Verse → Chorus → Outro (the ending section). The verse and the chorus are not the same chords. Verse: Am · C, then F · C. Chorus: Dm · F, then C · G/B. No capo needed — the open shapes you already know are all it takes.
All layers are teaching arrangements at 60 BPM — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions. The backing track's metronome reads 144 BPM, but it's easiest to feel in half that (about 72) — and each chord lasts a full bar of that slower count, four beats, same as every layer below.

* This jam track is in A minor, so the open shapes you know play in tune with no capo. To match the exact pitch of Olivia's original recording, put a capo on the 1st fret.

This is the newest song in the course — and the one that starts the quietest. Find the guitar first: it's acoustic, clean, and close, like it's being played in the room with you. Listen once for the chords changing, once for how little else is happening at the start, and once for how the song keeps building all the way to its loudest, fullest ending.

Do it: listen to the song (the clean edit). Count the chord changes in one verse — how many different chords do you hear?
Respond: clean or distorted? Acoustic or electric? How is Olivia's guitar tone different from "Seven Nation Army"'s? Answer in a frame: "Olivia's tone is ___, and the Seven Nation Army tone is ___."
Connect: name another current song where the guitar feels this close and quiet — "___ feels just as close and quiet."
Check it when: you've answered the questions and can hum the main hook from memory.

Play it: four beats per note at 60 BPM — strike each root once per beat, a full bar before you move. Fingering: index at fret 1 (F), pinky on the dot at fret 5 (A); for the C the whole hand slides up so the index lands on fret 8. Say the note names out loud as you go. You've got it when: two clean laps (a lap = one full time through the loop) with the hand landing on fret 8 and back on fret 1 without looking down.
Stuck?
Two halves. First A and C: pinky on the fret-5 dot, then slide the hand up so the index is on fret 8. Then F and C: index on fret 1, index on fret 8. Join them when the jump back from fret 8 to fret 1 is automatic.
Level up
Play the same roots on the A string instead: A open, C at fret 3. The F stays on the low E at fret 1. Same names — a string cross instead of a jump.
Check it when: you can play it from memory at 60 BPM, no stops.

This is the whole song as power chords — one two-finger shape that slides everywhere it needs to go. Verse: A5 · C5, then F5 · C5. Chorus: D5 · F5, then C5 · G5. And here's the surprising part: play it quietly. Power chords don't have to be loud.

Why the last one is G5
The chord is written G/B — a G chord with a B in the bass. As a power chord you play G5 at fret 3, because a power chord built on B would add an F♯, and there's no F♯ in this song. If you want the bassline the record actually plays, use the single note B at fret 7 — that's the one-fret step down from the C at fret 8.
Play it: four beats per chord at 60 BPM — one strum per beat, a full bar each — soft down-strums. You've got it when: two laps clean AND soft — every change landing on its beat. Losing the shape on the move to fret 1? Lift both fingers together and land them together.
Stuck?
Play just the low E notes for a lap — 5 · 8 · 1 · 8 · 10 · 1 · 8 · 3 — then add the A-string finger back one chord at a time.
Level up
Play the whole bassline as single notes, with the record's real bass note under the last chord: A(5) · C(8) · F(1) · C(8) · D(10) · F(1) · C(8) · B(7). Then play one lap loud and one lap very softly — same shapes, two totally different songs.
Check it when: the loop runs at 60 BPM, every string ringing clean, no stops.

The song sits in A minor, so A minor pentatonic fits over every chord. Olivia's song is soft — so your solo has to be too. Start with three notes and leave gaps of silence between notes — the space is part of the music.

3
Hammer-ons and pull-offs
Same box, two new moves. A hammer-on (h): pick the first note, then tap the higher fret hard with your ring finger — two notes, one pick. A slide (/): pick once, keep pressing, glide up. Practice each move slowly until the second note sounds clearly, then add them to your three-note phrases — this is what makes a phrase sound like the recording.

The whole song as open chords — the shapes you know. The verse is Am · C, then F · C. The chorus changes to Dm · F, then C · G/B. No capo: play them in open position and you're in tune with the jam track. G/B is just a G sound with its B note in the bass. One full bar per chord — four beats — the same count as Layers 2 and 3. The record's 144 BPM click feels like a slower ~72, and each chord lasts a full bar of that slower pulse; speeding this up toward 70 BPM puts you right at the record's real pace.

Play it: down-strums, one full bar per chord, speeding up in steps: 60, then 65, then 70 BPM. You've got it when: two clean loops of each — verse and chorus — at 70 BPM, one full bar per chord, every change on beat 1, every string ringing clear.
Stuck?
Strum only beat 1 of each chord and travel on the silent beats. Buzzing usually means a finger isn't pressing right behind the fret.
Level up
Folk strum (D-D-U-U-D-U) on each chord, or trade loops with a partner soloing Layer 4 over you — together you're the whole track.

This is "the cure" in its native style — no strumming. The verse is a soft 8-note roll, p-i-m-a-m-i-m-i (thumb · index · middle · ring · middle · index · middle · index), two notes per beat, your thumb walking the bass note of each chord while your fingers stay parked on the G, B, and e strings.

Then walk the whole verse: thumb moves to the A string (fret 3) for C, then the D string (fret 3) for F, then back to the A string for C — i-m-a never leave G-B-e.

Stuck?
Fret Am and pluck p-i-m-a in order, one note per beat, before adding the roll-back m-i-m-i. Speed comes after the order is automatic.
