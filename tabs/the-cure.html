Song Journey

"the cure"

Olivia Rodrigo
🧵 Thread song — it appears across all 5 modules 0 of 5 layers ready

One song, five layers. Every module this semester, you come back to this song with a new skill — and it gets bigger every time. There's also a bonus Layer 6 with the song's fingerpicked verse, once you're ready for a harder challenge.

Song map: Verse (soft, guitar carries it) → Chorus (fuller, same chords) → Verse → Chorus → Outro (the ending section). The whole song lives in one chord family: Am · C · Dm · F · G/B — no capo needed; the open shapes you already know are all it takes.
All layers are teaching arrangements at 60 BPM — simplified for learning, not note-for-note transcriptions. The backing track's metronome reads 144 BPM, but it's easiest to feel in half that (about 72) — count two of those slower beats per chord, same as every layer below.

* This jam track is in A minor, so the open shapes you know play in tune with no capo. To match the exact pitch of Olivia's original recording, put a capo on the 1st fret.

This is the newest song in the course — and the one that starts the quietest. Find the guitar first: it's acoustic, clean, and close, like it's being played in the room with you. Listen once for the chords changing, once for how little else is happening at the start, and once for how the song keeps building all the way to its big ending.

Do it: listen to the song (the clean edit). Count the chord changes in one verse — how many different chords do you hear?
Respond: clean or distorted? Acoustic or electric? How is Olivia's guitar tone different from "Seven Nation Army"'s?
Connect: name another current song where the guitar feels this close and quiet.
Check it when: you've answered the questions and can hum the main hook from memory.

Every chord has a root note, and the roots make a bassline you can play right now: A · C · D · F · G. This line crosses between the A and E strings — your first song that makes you switch strings mid-phrase.

Play it: two beats per note at 60 BPM. Say the note names out loud as you go. You've got it when: two clean laps (a lap = one full time through the loop) crossing between the A and E strings without looking down.
Stuck?
Two halves: A C D on the A string, then F G on the E string. Join them when the string cross is automatic.
Level up

The teaching arrangement sits in A minor, so A minor pentatonic fits over every chord. Olivia's song is soft — so your solo has to be too. Start with three notes and leave gaps of silence between notes — the space is part of the music.

Dress the notes
Same box, fancier moves. A hammer-on (h): pick the first note, then tap the higher fret hard with your ring finger — two notes, one pick. A slide (/): pick once, keep pressing, glide up. Work each move slowly until it speaks, then sneak them into your three-note phrases — suddenly you sound like a record.

Now the whole progression as open chords — the shapes you know, Am, C, Dm, F, strummed straight through. No capo: play them in open position and you're in tune with the jam track. The G/B turnaround (a short chord move that leads back to the start) is just a G sound with its B note in the bass, closing the loop back to Am. Two beats per chord here — same felt count as Layers 2 and 3 — because the record's 144 BPM click feels like a slower ~72; speeding this up toward 70 BPM puts you right at that real pace.

The shapes you're strumming:

|  Am    C     |  Dm    F     |
   1  2  3  4     1  2  3  4
then G/B for 2 beats to turn it around, back to Am
Play it: down-strums, two beats per chord, speeding up in steps: 60, then 65, then 70 BPM. You've got it when: two clean loops at 70 BPM, every change on beat 1, every string ringing clear.
Stuck?
Strum only beat 1 of each chord and travel on the silent beats. Buzzing usually means a finger isn't pressing right behind the fret.
Level up
Folk strum (D-D-U-U-D-U) on each chord, or trade loops with a partner soloing Layer 4 over you — together you're the whole track.

This is "the cure" in its native style — no strumming. The verse is a soft 8-note roll, p-i-m-a-m-i-m-i (thumb · index · middle · ring · middle · index · middle · index), two notes per beat, your thumb walking the bass note of each chord while your fingers stay parked on the G, B, and e strings.

The four shapes your thumb walks:

Am arpeggio · p i m a m i m i (one bar · 4/4)
e |-----------0--------------|
B |--------1-----1-----1-----|
G |-----2-----------2-----2--|
D |--------------------------|
A |--0-----------------------|
E |--------------------------|
Play it: one bar of Am, two notes per beat at 60 BPM — p (open A) · i (A, G string fret 2) · m (C, B string fret 1) · a (open e) · m · i · m · i. Then walk the whole verse: thumb moves to the A string (fret 3) for C, then the D string for Dm (open) and F (fret 3) — i-m-a never leave G-B-e.

Stuck?
Fret Am and pluck p-i-m-a in order, one note per beat, before adding the roll-back m-i-m-i. Speed comes after the order is automatic.
Level up
Once Am is smooth, cycle the full Am · C · Dm · F loop, one bar each — the roll needs a full bar per chord, so each chord here lasts twice as long as in Layer 5's strummed loop. This IS how the song wants to be played.
